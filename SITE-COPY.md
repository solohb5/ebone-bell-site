### THE ORIGIN STORY

In 2012, Eboné F. Bell walked into a bookstore and picked up every LGBTQ publication she could find. Not one featured women who looked like her.

She could have complained. She could have waited for someone else to fix it. Instead, she went home and started Tagg Magazine — the first national publication for queer women. Within years, it became a platform for stories that had never been told, voices that had never been amplified.

But a magazine wasn't enough. She saw young queer women of color who couldn't afford college. So she created the Tagg Scholarship Fund. She saw LGBTQ students at her university with nowhere to gather. So she founded a student organization that's still running 20 years later.

This is who Eboné is: someone who sees what's missing and builds it.

Today, that instinct drives her work as a keynote speaker and facilitator. She's addressed more than 100 universities, corporations like Microsoft and Deloitte, and stages like the WorldPride 2025 Human Rights Conference. Forbes named her one of "5 Inspiring Black Entrepreneurs Changing Our World."

But the mission hasn't changed since 2012: create spaces where people who've felt invisible finally feel seen.
